Radar Charts

Several dimensions plotted on radiating scaled axes, for comparing profiles rather than magnitudes

Basic Radar Chart

Declare one <dc-radar-axis> per dimension and one <dc-radar-series> per thing being compared. Points bind to axes by label, so the order you write them in does not matter.

One Series

<dc-radar-chart width="500" height="500" max-value="100">
  <dc-radar-axis label="Speed"></dc-radar-axis>
  <dc-radar-axis label="Power"></dc-radar-axis>
  <dc-radar-axis label="Range"></dc-radar-axis>
  <dc-radar-axis label="Comfort"></dc-radar-axis>
  <dc-radar-series label="Model A">
    <dc-point value="80" label="Speed"></dc-point>
    <dc-point value="65" label="Power"></dc-point>
    <dc-point value="90" label="Range"></dc-point>
    <dc-point value="45" label="Comfort"></dc-point>
  </dc-radar-series>
</dc-radar-chart>

Comparing Two

<dc-radar-chart width="500" height="500" max-value="100">
  <!-- same axes -->
  <dc-radar-series label="Model A">...</dc-radar-series>
  <dc-radar-series label="Model B">...</dc-radar-series>
  <dc-legend position="bottom"></dc-legend>
</dc-radar-chart>

Series are translucent by default. Two opaque polygons would hide each other, and comparison is the only reason to draw a second one.

Per-Axis Scales

Give an axis its own min-value and max-value when its units differ from the rest. This is what makes a radar honest: one shared scale is only meaningful when every dimension measures the same kind of thing, which is rare.

Independent Domains

<dc-radar-chart width="500" height="500" max-value="100">
  <dc-radar-axis label="Speed (km/h)" max-value="250"></dc-radar-axis>
  <dc-radar-axis label="Power (hp)" max-value="500"></dc-radar-axis>
  <dc-radar-axis label="Economy (mpg)" max-value="60"></dc-radar-axis>
  <dc-radar-series label="Model A">
    <dc-point value="200" label="Speed (km/h)"></dc-point>
    <dc-point value="400" label="Power (hp)"></dc-point>
    <dc-point value="30" label="Economy (mpg)"></dc-point>
  </dc-radar-series>
</dc-radar-chart>

Values Shown

<dc-radar-chart width="500" height="500"
                max-value="100" show-value
                value-format="number 0">
  <!-- same axes and series as above -->
</dc-radar-chart>

Grid and Orientation

Rings can follow the axes as polygons or be drawn as circles, and the whole chart can be rotated or reversed.

Circular Rings

<dc-radar-chart width="500" height="500" max-value="100"
                grid-shape="circle" rings="4">
  <dc-grid stroke="#cbd5e1" stroke-dasharray="dotted"></dc-grid>
  <!-- same axes and series as above -->
</dc-radar-chart>

Rotated

<dc-radar-chart width="500" height="500" max-value="100"
                start-angle="-54">
  <!-- same axes and series as above -->
</dc-radar-chart>

Missing Values

A dimension a series has no reading for uses the same missing policy as lines and areas — and it means the corresponding thing here.

gap (default)

<dc-radar-series label="Model B" missing="gap">
  <dc-point value="55" label="Speed"></dc-point>
  <dc-point value="90" label="Power"></dc-point>
  <dc-point label="Range"></dc-point>
  <dc-point value="85" label="Comfort"></dc-point>
</dc-radar-series>

skip

<dc-radar-series label="Model B" missing="skip">
  <!-- same points -->
</dc-radar-series>

zero is available and rarely right. Pulling the vertex to the centre changes the whole silhouette, which is the chart's entire message — it lies more loudly here than it does on a line.
